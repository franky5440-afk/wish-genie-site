Frequently asked questions

Some data shows "—"

Wish Genie can only read the Health data you allow, and iOS doesn't tell apps which types you turned off. To change it, open the iPhone Settings app, tap Privacy & Security › Health › Wish Genie, and turn on the types you want. (If your iPhone was set to Chinese when you first allowed access, it's listed as 許願精靈.) Wish Genie doesn't appear under Apps in the Health app itself: that list only shows apps that write Health data, and Wish Genie only reads.

If you never allowed Health access (for example, you tapped "Not now" at the start), Wish Genie isn't in that Settings list yet. Tap "Allow Health access" on Wish Genie's home screen, or in Wish Genie's Settings › Health data, and iOS lists each type so you can pick.

Updated to 1.4 and new kinds of data (such as blood pressure or nutrition) show "—"? Tap "More Health data can be read" on the home screen and allow the new items. If an item still isn't turned on (some iOS versions close the list early), turn it on in Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › Wish Genie.

What does the message on a task mean?

When an export fails, the task shows one of these messages. Codes like HTTP 404 come from your server or the cloud service.

  • Genie isn't allowed to read Health data yet. Tap "Check Health access" on the task and allow access (see above).
  • iCloud Drive isn't available. Open the iPhone Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, and make sure you're signed in and iCloud Drive is on.
  • Please reconnect Dropbox / Google Drive / OneDrive. The sign-in expired or was removed. Tap "Reconnect" on the task and sign in again.
  • Dropbox / Google Drive / OneDrive refused the request (HTTP 401 or 403). Make sure you can still save files in that account (for example, it isn't read-only), or reconnect it.
  • Dropbox / Google Drive / OneDrive couldn't find the folder to save to (HTTP 404). If it keeps happening, reconnect the account.
  • Your Dropbox / Google Drive / OneDrive storage is full. Free up space in that account; the next run tries again.
  • Couldn't reach the server. Check the iPhone's internet connection. For a server on your home network, make sure you're on the same Wi-Fi and Local Network access is allowed (see Can I send to a server on my home network? below).
  • The server refused the request (HTTP 401 or 403). Your server didn't accept the request: check the API key or token in the destination's headers.
  • The address wasn't found (HTTP 404). Check the destination's URL.
  • The server tried to redirect (HTTP 3xx). Wish Genie doesn't follow redirects. Use the final address instead.
  • The server said the file is too large (HTTP 413). Raise your server's upload size limit, or split the task so each one sends fewer types.
  • The server asked us to slow down (HTTP 429), or the server ran into a problem (HTTP 5xx). A problem on the server side; the next run tries again. OneDrive sometimes returns HTTP 503 on the very first upload to an account; tap "Run now" to try again right away.
  • This destination's settings need fixing. Open the destination and check its fields.
  • A password or key is missing. Open the destination and enter the password or header value again.
  • Something went wrong. Genie will try again. An unexpected error. If it keeps happening, email us and tell us when it happened.

When do automatic exports run?

Automatic exports run in the background when iOS gives the app time, so the exact timing is decided by the system. Daily and weekly tasks send at the time you pick (weekly ones on the day you pick). A late run doesn't move the schedule, and missed runs are caught up once. Health data can't be read while your iPhone is locked, so a run that falls in that window waits. To catch up, open Wish Genie or tap "Catch up" on the widget; turn on "Remind me if it doesn't send" on a task to get a notification when it's an hour late. You can always tap "Export now" on a task.

Can I give my health data to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

Yes, in two ways. In the app (since 1.6): "Ask AI" sends a summary of your data and your questions to a model you pick (models from these companies and others are available) through your own OpenRouter account; you pay OpenRouter for what you use. It needs Basic or Premium. See Ask AI. As a file you share yourself: in manual export, choose "AI summary" under "What goes in the file?", pick 7, 30 or 90 days and Markdown, then tap "Create file and share" and share the file to your AI assistant app (or save it and upload it in that app). A task can also send an AI summary to your cloud every day or every week. The summary has daily averages, the change from the previous period, the lowest and highest day, daily values, sleep stages and workouts, in English. If you share it with an AI app, that company receives this health data. A Markdown summary starts with a short note asking the AI to look at trends, not to diagnose, and to suggest seeing a doctor; you can turn it off. The summary is not a medical record. See the user guide.

Will the AI company use what I send to train its models?

It depends on the model. When you use Ask AI, your summary and questions go to OpenRouter and to the company behind the model you chose, and their own privacy policies apply. Some of those companies may keep what you send and use it to train their models. In your OpenRouter account, open Settings › Privacy to turn off providers that may train on your data (there are separate switches for paid and free models). Some models may then be unavailable. Wish Genie itself never receives your data or your conversations.

What do the Ask AI messages mean?

  • Your OpenRouter balance is too low. Add credits on openrouter.ai (Settings › Credits), then try again.
  • Your OpenRouter connection no longer works. The key was deleted on openrouter.ai or stopped working. Tap "Connect again".
  • Too many requests right now. Wait a moment and try again. Free models hit this often.
  • This model isn't available right now. Pick another model or try again later.
  • The request was turned down. Try a shorter question, fewer days of data, or another model.
  • The model didn't answer. Ask again or pick another model.
  • Couldn't reach OpenRouter. Check your internet connection. A very long answer that takes more than two minutes can also end this way; try fewer days of data.

How do I stop using Ask AI?

In Wish Genie, open Settings › AI assistant. "Disconnect" removes the key from this iPhone; to delete it from your OpenRouter account too, tap "Open the OpenRouter Keys page" and delete the key named "Wish Genie" (the app can't do this for you). "Withdraw agreement" stops anything from being sent until you agree again, and "Delete all conversations" removes your saved conversations from this iPhone.

Can I export my workouts?

Yes, since 1.4. Choose "Workouts" (the last group) in a task or in manual export. Each workout is one row: its length in minutes, the activity (for example running or traditionalStrengthTraining), and, when they were recorded, distance, active energy, average and maximum heart rate, and whether it was indoors. Workout routes (GPS and location) aren't read. Hourly and daily values count the number of workouts and their total minutes, and an AI summary lists them per activity. See the user guide.

Why do hourly or daily files repeat values I already received?

Watches often sync to the Health app hours later, so a task with hourly or daily values sends the last 24 hours (hourly) or the last 7 days (daily) again on every run, with up-to-date numbers. If you store them, replace earlier values that have the same data type and start time instead of adding them again.

What does my server receive?

A POST request (HTTPS, or http on your home network) with a JSON, CSV or Markdown body, depending on what goes in the file and the format you picked. Each sample has a unique ID, so your server can safely ignore duplicates. Sleep is sent one row per segment with its stage: deep, rem, core, awake, inBed, or unspecified (asleep, but no stage was recorded). Workouts are one row per workout; in JSON the details are in a "workout" object, and a CSV file always ends with six columns: workout_activity, workout_distance_m, workout_energy_kcal, workout_avg_hr, workout_max_hr and workout_indoor (empty for other kinds of data).

Can I send to a server on my home network?

Yes. Besides HTTPS, the app accepts plain http:// for addresses on your own network: a name ending in .local (for example http://my-nas.local:8080), a private IP address (10.x.x.x, 172.16–31.x.x or 192.168.x.x), or localhost. Any other address needs HTTPS. The first time, iOS asks whether Wish Genie may connect to devices on your local network; tap Allow. If that first export fails while the question is on screen, tap "Export now" again. You can change this later in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.

How does the free trial work?

Open the plans screen and tap "Start 7-day free trial". Everything is unlocked for 7 days. It's free and never turns into a paid subscription. After it ends, widgets and charts stay free; manual export and Shortcuts need Basic, and automatic exports need Premium.

I bought a plan but it isn't unlocked

Open Settings in the app and tap "Restore Purchases". Make sure you're signed in with the same Apple Account you used to buy it.

How do I cancel Premium Yearly?

In iOS Settings, tap your name › Subscriptions › Wish Genie. Cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date.
